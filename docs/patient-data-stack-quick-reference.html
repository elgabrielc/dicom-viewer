HIPAA (45 CFR 164.524) gives patients the right to their records. The 21st Century Cures Act / ASTP/ONC rules provide the mechanisms: bulk EHI Export (170.315(b)(10)) and a FHIR-based Patient Access API (170.315(g)(10)). The Information Blocking Rule (45 CFR Part 171) prohibits interference. Around this core sits a technical stack (FHIR R4 + US Core + SMART + OAuth + USCDI), older standards still dominant (HL7 v2, CCDA, DIRECT), terminologies (LOINC, SNOMED, RxNorm, ICD-10, CPT), DICOM in a parallel imaging universe, and inter-organizational networks (Care Everywhere, Carequality, CommonWell, TEFCA).

ix.The DICOM Gap

Imaging operates in a parallel universe. PACS, not EHR, stores pixel data. File sizes (0.5–3 GB per study) make patient-direct access hard. No certified patient API for DICOM under HTI-1/HTI-2.

  • The technical standard exists: DICOMweb (WADO-RS, QIDO-RS, STOW-RS) — but no federal mandate
  • De facto patient access: imaging portals (PocketHealth, Ambra, LifeImage, PowerShare) or CD/USB
  • IHE PDI profile defines CD/USB packaging
  • Major PACS vendors: Sectra, Visage, GE, Philips, Fujifilm Synapse

x.Inter-Organizational Exchange

  • Care Everywhere (Epic-to-Epic): dominant given Epic's market position; largely CCDA under the hood
  • Carequality: cross-vendor framework run by Sequoia Project
  • CommonWell Health Alliance: Cerner-led; now a TEFCA QHIN
  • TEFCA: federal "network of networks" with designated QHINs (Epic Nexus, eHealth Exchange, Health Gorilla, CommonWell, KONZA, MedAllies, Kno2)
  • Regional HIEs: Healthix (NYC), CRISP (MD/DC), Manifest MedEx (CA), IHIE (IN), etc.

xi.The Product Landscape

Hospital EHR market share (~2024): Epic ~36% / Oracle Health ~25% / Meditech ~13% / CPSI ~5% / Others ~21%. Epic dominates large AMCs and IDNs (~70-80% in those segments) and represents 50%+ of US hospital admissions. This concentration shapes the entire ecosystem.

Consumer patient apps: Apple Health (iOS) · Google Health Connect (Android) · CommonHealth · Fasten OnPrem · OneRecord · PocketHealth.

Developer platforms: 1upHealth · Particle Health · Health Gorilla · Flexpa · Redox · Datavant · Metriport.

Ambulatory EHRs (small practice): Athenahealth · eClinicalWorks · NextGen · Practice Fusion · Elation Health · Kareo/Tebra · DrChrono · OpenEMR.

AI scribes (subject to HTI-1 DSI transparency): DAX Copilot · Abridge · Suki · Augmedix · DeepScribe · Epic ambient AI · Heidi · Freed.

xii.For Third-Party Health Tech

Building

SMART on FHIR development

  • Register with each EHR developer portal (fhir.epic.com, fhir.oracle.com, etc.)
  • Build against sandbox first; target US Core profiles, not bare FHIR
  • Handle terminology resolution (LOINC, SNOMED, RxNorm)
  • Expect implementation variance across vendors
HIPAA gap

HIPAA stops applying for patient-authorized apps

Governed by FTC Act Section 5, FTC Health Breach Notification Rule, state health data laws (WA, CA, NV, CT, OR), general privacy laws. Recent enforcement: GoodRx ($1.5M), BetterHelp ($7.8M), Premom, Cerebral ($7M).

Compliance certifications

Beyond HIPAA

HITRUST CSF (healthcare de facto) · SOC 2 Type II (enterprise) · ISO 27001 · FedRAMP (federal) · GDPR readiness. HITRUST: 12-18 months, $50K-$500K.

FDA

Software as a Medical Device

If you interpret data clinically or give recommendations, SaMD regulations may apply. HTI-1 Predictive DSI transparency adds an AI-specific layer.

xiii.For Small Private Practices

Compliance checklist

Under HIPAA

  • Privacy Officer + Security Officer designated
  • Security Risk Analysis (164.308(a)(1))
  • Administrative, physical, technical safeguards
  • 30-day access response (or shorter state)
  • BAAs with business associates
  • Workforce training
Common pitfalls

What to avoid

  • Excessive fees — $6.50 electronic safe harbor is safest
  • Requiring in-person pickup when electronic is feasible
  • Overly broad authorizations for self-direct access
  • Delays past 30 days without written extension
  • Refusing electronic delivery when requested
  • Withholding clinical notes (OpenNotes mandate)
Mitigations

Practical resources

Choose an EHR that handles certification machinery. Engage compliance consultant. Cyber insurance. MGMA resources. Outsource records requests (ChartRequest, MRO) if volume is high.

xiv.For Patients

Connect an app

How to get continuous access

  • iOS: Apple Health → Browse → Health Records → Add Account → search provider
  • Android: Google Health Connect or CommonHealth
  • Cross-platform: Fasten OnPrem (self-hosted) or OneRecord (cloud)
Records language

Template phrases

"I am requesting access to my protected health information in the designated record set under 45 CFR 164.524."

"Pursuant to 45 CFR 164.524(c)(3), I am directing you to send a copy of these records to [recipient]."

Escalation

If a provider stonewalls

  • Written follow-up referencing 30-day (or shorter state) window
  • OCR complaint at hhs.gov/ocr or 1-800-368-1019
  • ASTP/ONC info blocking complaint at healthit.gov
  • State Department of Health
  • State Attorney General
  • Attorney

xv.The Clinical Reality

Works well

Reliable

  • USCDI data via Patient Access API
  • Apple Health / Google Health Connect
  • Care Everywhere Epic-to-Epic
  • TEFCA growth
  • OCR Right of Access Initiative
  • OpenNotes mandate
  • DIRECT for referrals
Still broken

Incomplete

  • DICOM imaging — no certified API
  • Patient portal messages — not in API
  • Billing/financial — uneven
  • Scanned documents — only via EHI Export
  • Cross-provider history depth
  • Genomic data — uneven
  • Mental health — restricted
  • Audit logs — no patient access
  • AI-generated note provenance

Implementation quality varies. Certification provides a floor, not a quality guarantee. Some vendor FHIR endpoints are fast and complete; others are slow or partial. Production differs from sandbox. Write defensive code.
